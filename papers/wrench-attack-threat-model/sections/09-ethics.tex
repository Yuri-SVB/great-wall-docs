\section{Ethics and responsible disclosure}
\label{sec:ethics}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This is offensive-flavored security research with direct life-safety stakes: we
formally derive an \emph{assassination} incentive. We therefore state plainly why
publishing it is, on balance, protective.

\textbf{The incentive is disclosed, not created.} The Deadly Race is a property of
a situation a coercer already faces; naming it hands nothing to an attacker who,
by Kerckhoffs, is already assumed to know the protocol and the landscape. What
publication changes is that \emph{defenders} can now measure a design against the
bar and reject the ones that manufacture the incentive. We describe an incentive
and the failure modes of existing defenses --- not a targeting method, and no
operational how-to.

\textbf{Obscurity is not a sound reason to withhold.} Not publishing analyses of
attack vectors and the failure modes of deployed defenses is itself a bet on
obscurity, and a defense that works only while the adversary stays ignorant
provides something worse than defenselessness: \emph{a false sense of security}.
Empirically, the registry~\cite{lopp-attacks} and the accompanying economic
analysis~\cite{gw-justification} show what Kerckhoffs predicts --- wrench attacks
are frequent and rising, and the entrenched counsel (\emph{keep a low profile},
\emph{never disclose ownership}, \emph{deny if asked}) has not stemmed them.
Security knowledge diffuses to criminal actors regardless of academic reticence;
withholding it handicaps only the defenders.

\textbf{The quiet years compound --- a supply-side precedent.} The ecosystem has
already run this experiment on itself, on the \emph{generation} side of the key
lifecycle. A March-2021 firmware defect left a fallback software PRNG silently
live as the production entropy path of a major hardware wallet
(Coldcard)~\cite{block-coldcard-postmortem,coinkite-entropy}; pseudorandom output
passes every eyeball test, so the weakness was invisible to inspection, and it
stayed quiet for over five years --- until a first wave of sweeps drained
${\approx}\$70$M from ${\approx}1{,}200$ addresses in 41 minutes on 30 July
2026~\cite{coindesk-coldcard}. Two lessons transfer verbatim. The quiet years were
not obscurity \emph{working}: the loss was contracted the moment the defect
shipped, and every silent month enrolled more holders into the eventual sweep.
And there was no ``quiet fix'' even in principle --- a warning channel wide enough
to reach the holders is wide enough to reach the attacker. Wrench-resistance sits
in the same epistemic position as entropy health: a holder might not realise from
outside whether their custody design protects or endangers them, so collective
silence about deadly-race dangers compounds exposure the same way, while it too
reads as prudence.

\textbf{Framing and restraint.} We keep the framing on protecting at-risk
holders, disclose our own bounded residual honestly rather than overclaim, and
treat the designs we classify as legitimate clearances by the other route, not
targets.
