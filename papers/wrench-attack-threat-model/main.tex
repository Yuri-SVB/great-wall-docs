\begin{abstract}
Physical-coercion (``wrench'') attacks defeat custody schemes that key management
and cryptography treat as out of scope, and the countermeasures the industry has
shipped against them have never been assessed against a common bar. We supply
one, and use it to systematize the design space. We give a formal model of
coercion against a secret-holder and prove that a large class of ``graceful''
recovery designs --- those that leave a device seizure holding a
\emph{feasible-but-slower} path to the secret --- are strictly \emph{worse than
useless}: they manufacture a material incentive to \emph{assassinate} the victim.
We name this the \textbf{Deadly Race}, derive it from a single impossibility (one
cannot prove one has forgotten), and distill from it a design bar --- the
\textbf{No-Gray-Area Criterion} --- with a \emph{reachability} dichotomy that
partitions every way of clearing it, and a trichotomy that enumerates the
mechanisms available to a design that tries. Against that taxonomy we classify
what is actually deployed: time-lock-rescue vaults clear the bar honestly, by
putting the winning racer beyond the attacker's reach, at the cost of a trusted
third party; the standard industry control set partitions cleanly into the
configurations that clear it and those that are worse than their own absence; and
the most widely shipped consumer countermeasure, the self-destruct
(``wallet-erase'') PIN, clears nothing --- the vendor's own documentation
presupposes a surviving backup, so the erasure removes the device path while
leaving intact the path that runs through the holder. Two commonly held
assumptions do not survive the exercise: that discretion about ownership is a
security mechanism, and that a duress PIN is a coercion defense. A construction,
the \textbf{Time-Gated Perceptual Oracle}, shows the bar is reachable by the other
route with custody kept \emph{individual}. Coding of $352$ incidents from the
physical-attack registry grounds the model's stipulations, attests the predicted
terminal outcome, and bounds the delegated route empirically. The framework fixes
a bar; it does not claim this construction is the unique way to clear it.

\keywords{Bitcoin self-custody \and physical-coercion (wrench) attacks \and
coercion resistance \and Kerckhoffs's principle \and threat modeling.}
\end{abstract}
