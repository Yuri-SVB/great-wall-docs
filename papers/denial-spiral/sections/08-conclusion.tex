\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The advice to deny is not merely weak. It is a claim on a resource that other
holders supply, and spending it lowers what every future denial is worth ---
including the denial of someone who bought nothing, trained for nothing, and in
the limiting case holds nothing. That is the Denial Spiral, and it is why the
obscurity class cannot be assessed one holder at a time.

The class as shipped fails on its vendors' own documentation. A wallet-erase PIN
presupposes the backup that defeats it; a decoy wallet is sold and coached for
credibility, which is the naive tier stated as a training objective; and at least
one vendor attaches ``plausible deniability'' to a storage-location flag whose
purpose is documented in public.

What follows is not a prediction that the field will now correct itself. Three
mechanisms censor the record and a fourth censors the response: conceding costs
whoever concedes, while recalcitrance costs holders who are not in the room. The
prediction we do make is narrower and is the one worth testing --- that the
availability of this analysis will not, by itself, produce its adoption, and that
adoption will arrive first wherever conceding is made cheap: a neutral weakness
class to cite, a reviewed venue to cite it from, and a bar defined before anyone
is measured against it.

The bar itself is in the companion~\cite{deadly-race}. This paper's contribution
is the reason the bar had to be derived rather than measured, and the mechanism
that will keep it from being measured after publication.
