\section{The Denial Spiral}
\label{sec:spiral}
%% ==========================================================================

\begin{remark}[The Denial Spiral]\label{rem:denial-spiral}
The paper has now met two distinct ways a custody practice can worsen its
holder's position --- the Deadly Race, borrowed in \S\ref{sec:model} and derived
in the companion, and one that still needs a name.

Denial credibility is a \emph{common resource}: it is supplied by the population of
holders and consumed by each holder who denies. Under the adversary of
Assumption~\ref{ass:power}, a norm of concealment and coached denial depletes it. Where
denial is the expected script a denial carries no information, so an interrogation that
opens with one yields $\Attacker$ no exculpatory update and its rational continuation is
further coercion rather than release (Corollary~\ref{cor:disclosure}). The individual
holder's rational response to that environment --- conceal harder, rehearse better ---
depletes the resource further, for everyone, including the truthful denier who bought
nothing (Remark~\ref{rem:decoy-market}).

We call this the \textbf{Denial Spiral}. It is the Obscurity Paradox
(Remark~\ref{rem:obscurity-paradox}) run in reverse, and it is the companion of the
Deadly Race rather than a special case of it: the Race prices the \emph{terminal} move,
the Spiral prices the \emph{duration} --- the abductions, long holds and torture the
registry codes (\S\ref{sec:registry}). A design can be innocent of one and guilty of
the other, which is why they are named apart.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[The Spiral is not a sixth axis either]\label{cor:spiral-equiv}
Whether a design \emph{depletes} the denial commons is equivalent to its failing
Property~\ref{p:nonob}. A design whose operation requires the holder to deny --- a
decoy, a concealed erasure --- adds a scripted denial to the population and lowers what
any denial is worth; a design that does not, and whose specification is public, does the
reverse, since a denial made from within it is consistent with an ordinary setup rather
than being the script (Remark~\ref{rem:obscurity-paradox}). Depletion therefore tracks
non-obscurity exactly, and a Denial Spiral column would reproduce that one as surely as
a no-hostage column would reproduce Property~\ref{p:know}.

\emph{Exposure} is a different matter, and it is not a property of any design. The
Spiral's cost is ambient: it falls on whoever must be believed, including the holder of
a design that depletes nothing, and including the person who, on his own account,
held no bitcoin at all --- the RATIRL case (\S\ref{par:ratirl}), and the reason the
externality is worth naming. The qualifier is owed: \S\ref{par:ratirl} records that the
denial may itself have been defensive, and the externality argument is the branch on
which it was true. No column can carry that, because no column has a row for the people it
lands on.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{``Take me with you'' --- the Scheherazadian bargain}
\label{sub:scheherazade}

\noindent What follows is a \textbf{conjecture}, not a result. Nothing else in
this paper rests on it, there is no empirical evidence confirming any particular
instance of the chain of events below, and it is set down here rather than
withheld because the silence around it is over-determined by this paper's own
\S\ref{sec:anosognosia}: a headline recording that a holder was held for eleven
hours, doesn't necessarily specify whether those hours went on interrogation, 
coercing relatives through threat against the captive holder, or on waiting 
for a path to clear.

\begin{remark}[The Scheherazadian bargain]\label{rem:scheherazade}
At any point in an encounter on a Deadly-Race-vulnerable setup, $\Attacker$,
$\Victim$ or both may work out Lemma~\ref{lem:drl}. A $\WDYbay$ attacker
(Definition~\ref{def:belief}) who has updated his way to being satisfied that he
holds everything may \emph{still} not release, because, by NNPP, any released holder
is potentially a competitor; detention buys the same assurance as the terminal
move at a fraction of the judicial disutility. Gray-area vulnerability
therefore prices \textbf{custody
as assurance of $\Victim$'s non interference}. The conjecture is that $\Victim$,
having run the same arithmetic, will \emph{volunteer} for it: \emph{take me with
you, and you will know I am not racing you}.

We call it the \textbf{Scheherazadian bargain}. In the Arabian classic \emph{One 
Thousand and One Nights} the protagonist volunteers for what turns out to be 
a captivity in order to stop the killing of third parties, then prolongs that
captivity night by night, a thousand and one times, to stave off her own, bidding
cliffhanger resolutions for her life.

In the Deadly Race, the bargain consists of changing \emph{killing} for \emph{captivity}
in one case $\Victim$'s and $\Attacker$'s interests align: $\Victim$ keeps his life,
and $\Attacker$ collects the pivotality increment $\pi$ that the terminal move would
have bought him (Lemma~\ref{lem:drl}) --- a $\Victim$ in custody is a $\Victim$ not
racing --- at a (judicial, moral, social) disutility below $c$. What $\Victim$ supplies
is simply being held.
Obviously, the bargain also inevitably incurs in longer coercion that may result in
a $\Victim$--to--$\Attacker$ value extraction greater than in case $\Victim$
was released.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The analogy holds on two things and nothing else.}
First, volunteering into custody to avert a killing. The motives differ --- hers
is altruistic, the hostage's is his own skin --- but at the level of \emph{enter
custody so that nobody is killed}. Second, prolonging a custody for avoiding terminal
move now, in exchange for something. This is the clause worth stating, because it
names what the captor is buying: the classic's villain, king Shahryar, buys the end
of the night's story, the attacker buys a lower judicial liability while retaining
about as much race advantage.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Two things the model settles before the bargain is
reached.} First, the delegate. One may object that custody cannot give
$\Attacker$ what he is buying, since by Assumption~\ref{lem:nnpl} he can never
rule out an access-dominating remote delegate. The companion resolves that
belief conservatively and generally, not here: unless dominance has been
\emph{demonstrated}, $\Attacker$ proceeds as though there is no
delegate~\cite[Rem.~6]{deadly-race}. The simplification maximizes his assessed
pivotality and so the assessed payoff of every move the Lemma prices --- the
terminal move and detention alike --- which is why it is \emph{a fortiori} for
the bar rather than a convenience for this conjecture.

Second, relevance of $\Victim$'s preference. The adversary is granted
complete subjugation and $\Victim$'s full cooperation~\cite[\S2]{deadly-race},
so in the idealized model an abduction happens if and only if $\Attacker$
chooses one, and what $\Victim$ would prefer changes nothing. The practical relevance
of $\Victim$'s preference means something only as one moves toward the real encounter.

Consider the hypothetical example below:

$\Victim$: "Please do kill me. I beg of you (...). There is a better way: Take me with 
you so you will know I'll not abort the transaction nor send distress signal. I'll help.
Exit the building through exit B, not A, to avoid detection. I'll turn off the alarm."
$\Attacker$ then proceed as suggested, while keeping the terminal move readily available,
carries out suggested exit plan, which he verifies happened smoothly without triggering
(apparent) alarm or detection.

That would be an example of succesful Scheherazadian bargain, particularly on in which
$\Victim$'s preference might have been relevant. $\Victim$ volunteered
intel (exit B is more discrete; there is an alarm; alarm can be disabled) that a realistic
attacker, in the chaos, and adrenaline of a real wrench attack, might have otherwise been operationally
unable to be aware of, consider, extract from victim, evaluate and act upon; and which turned abduction
instead of killing a more feasible alternative.

\medskip\noindent The route, however, does not escape the Spiral. It feeds into
it by a second road: the abduction's duration is epistemic deadlock, where the
interrogation cannot end because nothing settles whether there is more, this is
a strategic hostage problem; where $\Attacker$ may be satisfied and detain
anyway, it's the Scheherazadian alternative to enacting the Deadly Race.
Both produce the same observable, and \S\ref{sec:anosognosia}
mechanisms are why no registry column will ever separate them.
\end{remark}
