\section{The Denial Spiral}
\label{sec:spiral}
%% ==========================================================================

\begin{remark}[The Denial Spiral]\label{rem:denial-spiral}
The paper has now met two distinct ways a custody practice can worsen its
holder's position --- the Deadly Race, borrowed in \S\ref{sec:model} and derived
in the companion, and one that still needs a name.

Denial credibility is a \emph{common resource}: it is supplied by the population of
holders and consumed by each holder who denies. Under the adversary of
Assumption~\ref{ass:power}, a norm of concealment and coached denial depletes it. Where
denial is the expected script a denial carries no information, so an interrogation that
opens with one yields $\Attacker$ no exculpatory update and its rational continuation is
further coercion rather than release (Corollary~\ref{cor:disclosure}). The individual
holder's rational response to that environment --- conceal harder, rehearse better ---
depletes the resource further, for everyone, including the truthful denier who bought
nothing (Remark~\ref{rem:decoy-market}).

We call this the \textbf{Denial Spiral}. It is the Obscurity Paradox
(Remark~\ref{rem:obscurity-paradox}) run in reverse, and it is the companion of the
Deadly Race rather than a special case of it: the Race prices the \emph{terminal} move,
the Spiral prices the \emph{duration} --- the abductions, long holds and torture the
registry codes (\S\ref{sec:registry}). A design can be innocent of one and guilty of
the other, which is why they are named apart.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[The Spiral is not a sixth axis either]\label{cor:spiral-equiv}
Whether a design \emph{depletes} the denial commons is equivalent to its failing
Property~\ref{p:nonob}. A design whose operation requires the holder to deny --- a
decoy, a concealed erasure --- adds a scripted denial to the population and lowers what
any denial is worth; a design that does not, and whose specification is public, does the
reverse, since a denial made from within it is consistent with an ordinary setup rather
than being the script (Remark~\ref{rem:obscurity-paradox}). Depletion therefore tracks
non-obscurity exactly, and a Denial Spiral column would reproduce that one as surely as
a no-hostage column would reproduce Property~\ref{p:know}.

\emph{Exposure} is a different matter, and it is not a property of any design. The
Spiral's cost is ambient: it falls on whoever must be believed, including the holder of
a design that depletes nothing, and including the person who, on his own account,
held no bitcoin at all --- the RATIRL case (\S\ref{par:ratirl}), and the reason the
externality is worth naming. The qualifier is owed: \S\ref{par:ratirl} records that the
denial may itself have been defensive, and the externality argument is the branch on
which it was true. No column can carry that, because no column has a row for the people it
lands on.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{``Take me with you'' --- the Scheherazadian bargain}
\label{sub:scheherazade}

\noindent What follows is a \textbf{conjecture}, not a result. Nothing else in
this paper rests on it, there is no empirical evidence confirming any particular
instance of the chain of events below, and it is set down here rather than
withheld because the silence around it is over-determined by this paper's own
\S\ref{sec:anosognosia}: a headline records that a holder was held for eleven
hours, never whether those hours went on interrogation or on waiting for a path
to clear.

\begin{remark}[The Scheherazadian bargain]\label{rem:scheherazade}
At any point in an encounter on a Deadly-Race-vulnerable setup, $\Attacker$,
$\Victim$ or both may work out Lemma~\ref{lem:drl}. A $\WDYbay$ attacker
(Definition~\ref{def:belief}) who has updated his way to being satisfied that he
holds everything may \emph{still} not release, because a released holder with a
feasible path is a competitor; detention buys the same assurance as the terminal
move at a fraction of the judicial disutility. Gray-area vulnerability
therefore prices \textbf{custody
as collateral on $\Victim$'s own abstention}. The conjecture is that $\Victim$,
having run the same arithmetic, will \emph{volunteer} for it: \emph{take me with
you, and you will know I am not racing you}.

We call it the \textbf{Scheherazadian bargain}. In the \emph{One Thousand and One
Nights} the protagonist volunteers for what turns out to be a captivity in order
to stop the killing of third parties, then prolongs that captivity night by
night, a thousand and one times, to stave off her own, bidding cliffhanger
resolutions for her life.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{What is traded is non-racing, and nothing else.} The
bargain must not be run together with a second thing the same captivity makes
possible: that more hours of coercion may extract more than a released $\Victim$
would ever have surrendered. That second thing is real, and it is this paper's
duration mechanism, but in it $\Victim$ and $\Attacker$ want opposite things.
If $\Victim$ volunteers he does so despite that, because it is his life at
stake, not because of it. The bargain is the one case in which their
interests \emph{align}: $\Victim$ keeps his life, and $\Attacker$ collects the
pivotality increment $\pi$ that the terminal move would have bought him
(Lemma~\ref{lem:drl}) --- a $\Victim$ in custody is a $\Victim$ not racing ---
at a judicial disutility below $c$. Nothing is disclosed to buy it. What
$\Victim$ supplies is simply being held.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The analogy holds on two things and nothing else.}
First, volunteering into custody to avert a killing. The motives differ --- hers
is altruistic, the hostage's is his own skin --- but at the level of \emph{enter
custody so that nobody is killed} the move is the same, so the difference in
motive costs the name nothing. Second, custody accepted in place of the terminal
move now, in exchange for something. This is the clause worth stating, because it
names what the captor is buying: the king is buying the end of the story, the
attacker the same race advantage at a lower judicial price.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Two things the model settles before the bargain is
reached.} First, the delegate. One may object that custody cannot give
$\Attacker$ what he is buying, since by Assumption~\ref{lem:nnpl} he can never
rule out an access-dominating remote delegate. The companion resolves that
belief conservatively and generally, not here: unless dominance has been
\emph{demonstrated}, $\Attacker$ proceeds as though there is no
delegate~\cite[Rem.~6]{deadly-race}. The simplification maximizes his assessed
pivotality and so the assessed payoff of every move the Lemma prices --- the
terminal move and detention alike --- which is why it is \emph{a fortiori} for
the bar rather than a convenience for this conjecture.

Second, $\Victim$'s willingness is not a free variable. The adversary is granted
complete subjugation and $\Victim$'s full cooperation~\cite[\S2]{deadly-race},
so in the idealized model an abduction happens if and only if $\Attacker$
chooses one, and what $\Victim$ would prefer changes nothing. The volunteering
is therefore not a mechanism of that model. It means something only as one moves
toward the real encounter --- a $\WDYbay$ attacker whose assessment can be
moved, and an offer that is information rather than consent --- which is why the
conjecture lives in this paper and in this regime.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{What it buys, and what that costs.}

\emph{(i) It does buy a reprieve, and the reprieve is bounded.} Write $R$
for the racer set and $p_A(S)$ for $\Attacker$'s probability of prevailing
against racers $S$ (\cite[Def.~6]{deadly-race}), and $\pi := p_A(R \setminus
\{\Victim\}) - p_A(R)$ for $\Victim$'s pivotality. Lemma~\ref{lem:drl} prices
the terminal move as $(1-p_A)\,W > c$; the two forms agree under individual
custody, where removing $\Victim$ leaves no racer at all and $\pi = 1 - p_A$,
and $\pi$ is the form to use wherever another racer survives. Let $\kappa$ be
what a hold costs $\Attacker$ per period --- logistics, custody, exposure ---
with no time preference modeled. The three moves then separate:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcl@{}}
\toprule
$\Attacker$'s move & win probability & cost \\
\midrule
hold $\Victim$ for $T$ & $p_A(R \setminus \{\Victim\})$ & $\kappa T$, and still running \\
terminal move & $p_A(R \setminus \{\Victim\})$ & $c$, once \\
release $\Victim$ & $p_A(R)$ & $0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The first two buy the \emph{same} win probability and differ only in
price and in tenure: \textbf{detention rents what the terminal move buys
outright.} There are three pairwise comparisons, not two, and they give the hold
two horizons:
\[
  \text{hold beats release} \iff \kappa T < \pi W,
  \qquad
  \text{hold beats the terminal move} \iff \kappa T < c .
\]
Nothing obliges $\Attacker$ to hold. He holds where and for as long as his own
arithmetic says so, against the earlier of $T^{\dagger} = \pi W/\kappa$ and
$T^{*} = c/\kappa$, and the Lemma's own condition is exactly the switch between
them: $\pi W > c$ iff $T^{*} < T^{\dagger}$. The \emph{bargain} --- $\Victim$
volunteering --- is meaningful only in that half, because only there is there a
killing for him to avert. In the other half $\Attacker$ may still detain, but on
his own account, with nothing offered and nothing agreed.

\medskip\noindent Where he does hold, $\Victim$ is alive who would otherwise be
dead, and that is a real reprieve. It is also a bounded one: it runs at most to
$T^{*}$, past which the terminal move is strictly cheaper for an identical
result, and absent new information an $\Attacker$ who expects at the outset to
hold past $T^{*}$ does better to enact the terminal move at once. Its length is
set in $\Attacker$'s cost parameters, not in anything $\Victim$ holds.

There is one end to the hold that is not the terminal move: $\Attacker$
completing the spend, after which $\pi = 0$ and release is free. But its arrival
is $\Attacker$'s residual latency on his own path, which $\Victim$ cannot hasten
once held --- and it is the \emph{only} such end, because by
Assumption~\ref{lem:nnpl} $\Attacker$'s posterior on an undisclosed path never
reaches $0$, so release never becomes free on the evidence $\Victim$ supplies.
Note the regime: this is the Bayesian reading, in which the posterior moves but
cannot be driven to zero, not the dogmatic one in which nothing moves it at all
(Definition~\ref{def:belief}).

\emph{(ii) The reprieve is from a danger the gray band created, which is the
charge against the band and not a defense of it.} A design clearing
Criterion~\ref{prin:nga} leaves no racer to remove, hence no terminal move to be
reprieved from, hence nothing for a kidnapping to substitute for. The available
misreading --- that a gray outcome may therefore save a life --- gets the sign
right and the baseline wrong. Against the bar, the band buys $\Victim$ at most
$c/\kappa$ and bills him a kidnapping for it.

$\Victim$ has no lever on either term. Scheherazade's payment is renewable and
hers to withhold, which is how she reaches night $1001$; what $\Victim$ supplies
is supplied by $\Attacker$'s own act of holding him, so there is nothing for him
to withhold and nothing to renegotiate with. In the book she is pardoned on the
thousand and second morning; the registry (\S\ref{sec:registry}) records no
counterpart to that morning.

\medskip\noindent The route therefore does not escape the Spiral. It feeds into
it by a second road: the abduction's duration is epistemic deadlock, where the
interrogation cannot end because nothing settles whether there is more; this is
a strategic hostage problem, where $\Attacker$ may be satisfied and detain
anyway. Both produce the same observable, and \S\ref{sec:anosognosia}'s first
mechanism is why no registry column will ever separate them.
\end{remark}
